## Learning

One of the things I enjoy most is learning difficult subject independently or with a friend.

My German learning has largely been a self directed process. I experimented with a range of recommended apporoaches and kept the techniques that resulted in measurable progress. I utilized a tutor during my A2/B1 learning to assist with speech and grammer development. From there I progressed to a sucessfull Goethe B2 exam independently with excellent grades including 100% in speaking.

I think it's interesting to note down what I did in case it helps anyone else.

I approach my work in traumatology and as a medic in much the same way. Alongside day-to-day clinical work, I try to build a structured understanding through a combination of mentor discussions, practical experience and study from comprehensive references such as Rockwood and Green's Fractures in Adults, as well as concise guideline oriented handbooks such as the Oxford Clinical Handbook series.

I have experimented with programming on and off since high-school, I saw the potential in writing custom software but the serious need for it only began to arise in the course of my research.

My thesis work was in part a literature review which involved coallate the findings across hundreds of research papers. To make this manageable, I wrote a custom tool that allowed me to tag, categorize, and develop a hierarchical structure for the outcomes and methods associated with each paper.
It made it very easy to generate correct results afterwards and tweak the pipeline as needed.

I also then completed the HardvardX CS50 course and written software, libraries, and used programming languages to typeset forms.

My thesis work involved writing a tool to coallate the findings of hundreds of research papers. Since then I have completed the HarvardX CS50 course and written software, libraries, and used programming languages to typeset forms

For me, learning is less about hoarding information than building a framework that allows new knowledge to find a place.

I intentionally limited my use of AI for all these tasks associated with learning. The problem as it stands with what's
commerically avaialble and what a single person uses but the tests are also primed to always succeed and not really test
the logic. As it stands, it's for exploratory programming work and understanding what's available in the solution space
but not for the final code. Also, especially for the things that are on my own I want to understand the code as I continue
learning.
